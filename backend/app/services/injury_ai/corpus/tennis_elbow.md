# Lateral Epicondylalgia (Tennis Elbow)

Lateral epicondylalgia is an overuse degenerative tendinopathy of the extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB) origin at the lateral humerus epicondyle.

## Mechanism and Symptoms
- Point tenderness 1-2 cm distal to the lateral epicondyle.
- Pain aggravated by resisted wrist extension, resisted middle finger extension (Maudsley's test), and gripping objects (rackets, dumbbells, door handles).
- Caused by excessive wrist flicking, late backhands, improper grip size, or sudden increases in court volume.

## Progressive Loading Protocol (Alfredson Variation)
1. **Isometric Stage**: Sustained wrist extension holds against gentle resistance (30-45 seconds, 5 repetitions, 3x daily). Provides immediate pain inhibition.
2. **Eccentric-Concentric Stage**: The Tyler Twist exercise using a flexible resistance bar (FlexBar) has demonstrated dramatic pain reduction and functional improvement.
3. **Forearm Extensor Release**: Soft-tissue mobilization of the forearm extensor muscle belly, avoiding heavy direct friction on the bony insertion during active flare-ups.
4. **Equipment Modification**: Racket grip size adjustment (distance from palm crease to tip of ring finger should match grip perimeter), string tension reduction by 3-5 lbs, and vibration dampeners.
