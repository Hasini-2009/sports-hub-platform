# Subacromial Shoulder Impingement and Rotator Cuff Tendinopathy

Subacromial pain syndrome (shoulder impingement) is widespread among athletes performing repetitive overhead motions (swimming, tennis serves, badminton smashes, volleyball spikes).

## Pathology
Compression of the supraspinatus tendon, subacromial bursa, and long head of biceps tendon under the coracoacromial arch during overhead elevation and internal rotation.

## Clinical Tests
- **Neer's Sign**: Passive elevation with internal rotation creates anterior shoulder pain.
- **Hawkins-Kennedy**: Forward flexion to 90 degrees followed by forceful passive internal rotation.
- **Empty Can Test (Jobe)**: Arm elevated at 90 degrees in scapular plane with thumb pointed downward against downward resistance.

## Corrective Protocols
1. **Scapular Dyskinesis Correction**: Strengthen serratus anterior (scapular push-ups, wall slides) and lower trapezius (prone Y-raises).
2. **Rotator Cuff Conditioning**: High-repetition, low-load external rotations using resistance bands (in neutral and 90-degree abduction).
3. **Posterior Shoulder Mobility**: Sleeper stretch or cross-body stretch to relieve posterior capsule tightness which otherwise causes anterosuperior humeral head migration.
4. **Technique Assessment**: Assess kinetic chain transfer during overhead strikes. Power should originate from hips and trunk rotation rather than isolated arm pulling.
