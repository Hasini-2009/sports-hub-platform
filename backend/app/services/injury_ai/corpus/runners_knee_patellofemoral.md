# Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome (Runner's Knee in Track & Field)

Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome (PFPS), commonly known as runner's knee, is an overload condition characterized by diffuse retro-patellar or peri-patellar anterior knee pain, prevalent in track sprinters, middle-distance runners, and jumpers.

## Clinical Presentation
- Dull, aching pain behind or around the kneecap.
- Exacerbated by loading the flexed knee: stair descent, squatting, lunges, and prolonged sitting with bent knees ('theater sign').
- Negative for joint effusion or mechanical locking (which would indicate meniscus or ligament injury).

## Biomechanical Causes
- Deficient gluteus medius and maximus strength leading to dynamic knee valgus and excessive internal femoral rotation.
- Weakness in the quadriceps (vastus medialis oblique - VMO).
- Over-striding during sprinting: landing with foot excessively anterior to the body's center of mass increases peak patellofemoral joint reaction forces.

## Evidence-Based Management
1. **Targeted Hip and Gluteal Strengthening**:
   - Resisted side-lying clamshells, monster walks with mini-bands, and single-leg Romanian deadlifts. Combining hip-focused exercise with quadriceps training yields superior outcomes compared to quad exercises alone.
2. **Step Rate / Cadence Modification**:
   - Increasing running cadence by 7-10% without altering speed reduces peak patellofemoral joint stress by up to 20%.
3. **Load Management**:
   - Temporary reduction of downhill running and excessive plyometric volume. Replace high-impact running with non-impact cross-training (elliptical, swimming) during acute symptom flare-ups.
