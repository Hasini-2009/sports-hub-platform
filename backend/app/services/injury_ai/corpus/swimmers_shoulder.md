# Swimmer's Shoulder (Subacromial Impingement in Competitive Aquatics)

Swimmer's shoulder is the most prevalent musculoskeletal condition affecting competitive swimmers, with prevalence rates ranging between 40% and 91% across collegiate and elite Olympic swimmers.

## Biomechanics & Mechanism
A competitive swimmer performs between 2,500 and 4,000 shoulder revolutions per session. Repetitive arm elevation in internal rotation (freestyle and butterfly recovery and pull phases) narrows the subacromial space, causing mechanical abrasion against the supraspinatus tendon, long head of biceps, and subacromial bursa.

## Risk Factors
- Scapular dyskinesis (drooping or anterior tilting of the scapula).
- Hypermobility with secondary glenohumeral anterior laxity.
- Muscle fatigue in serratus anterior, lower trapezius, and posterior rotator cuff.
- Excessive crossing over the midline during hand entry.

## Prevention & Rehabilitation Protocols
1. **Scapular Stabilization**:
   - Y-to-W progressions with resistance bands.
   - Serratus anterior punches and wall slides with foam roller.
2. **Dynamic Warm-Up**:
   - Dryland band warm-up prior to entering water: 2x15 external rotations, band pull-aparts, and dynamic chest openers. Never stretch cold shoulders passively before practice.
3. **Core Kinetic Chain Linking**:
   - Integrate body roll through thoracic spine and hip rotation. Power must initiate from hip torque rather than purely isolated shoulder exertion.
4. **Stroke Mechanics Refinement**:
   - Avoid thumb-first entry (places arm in extreme internal rotation); ensure fingertip or flat-hand entry with fingertips aligned forward.
