# Medial Tibial Stress Syndrome (MTSS / Shin Splints)

Medial Tibial Stress Syndrome (MTSS) is one of the most frequent lower extremity overuse injuries in runners, basketball, football, and racquet athletes.

## Differential Diagnosis: MTSS vs. Tibial Stress Fracture
- **MTSS (Shin Splints)**: Diffuse pain along the posteromedial border of the lower third of the tibia (>5 cm continuous area). Tenderness diminishes with warm-up in early stages.
- **Tibial Stress Fracture**: Focal point tenderness (<2-3 cm) along the anterior or medial cortex. Night pain, pain on low-impact walking, and positive 'hop test' (severe focal pain on single-leg hopping). If focal tenderness or night pain is present, immediately stop impact exercise and arrange a targeted MRI/bone scan.

## Management Protocol
1. **Load Modification**: Decrease impact training volume by 50% or substitute with non-impact cross-training (deep water running, swimming, stationary bike) until baseline walking is pain-free.
2. **Tibialis Posterior & Soleus Strengthening**: Bent-knee seated calf raises (targets soleus), resisted foot inversion with band, toe curling with towel.
3. **Footwear and Biomechanics**: Check for excessive foot pronation; semi-rigid orthotic arch supports or motion-control shoes reduce eccentric strain on the deep posterior compartment.
4. **Cadence Manipulation**: Increasing running step rate by 5-10% significantly reduces peak tibial impact shock and ground reaction forces.
