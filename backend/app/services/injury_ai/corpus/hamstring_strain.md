# Hamstring Strain Injury Prevention and Rehabilitation

Hamstring strains (biceps femoris long head most frequent) occur predominantly during terminal swing phase in high-speed sprinting or extensive lengthening movements (kicking, lunging).

## Risk Factors
- Previous hamstring injury (highest predictor).
- Asymmetry in eccentric hamstring strength (hamstring-to-quadriceps ratio <0.60).
- Poor lumbo-pelvic core stability and anterior pelvic tilt.
- Fatigue during the final 15 minutes of match halves.

## Prevention Protocols: The Nordic Hamstring Curl
Incorporating the Nordic Hamstring Exercise into regular training reduces hamstring strain rates by up to 51% across competitive sports:
- Protocol: 2-3 sessions per week during preseason, 1 session per week during in-season maintenance.
- Focus on slow, controlled eccentric descent keeping hips fully extended.

## Rehabilitation Progression
- **Acute Phase (Days 1-5)**: Isometric knee flexions at varying angles (30, 60, 90 degrees), pain-free bridges on floor, low-cadence cycling. Avoid excessive stretching into pain.
- **Sub-acute (Weeks 1-3)**: The 'Askling L-Protocol' (The Extender, The Diver, The Glider) focusing on lengthening contractions. Single-leg Romanian deadlifts (RDLs).
- **Functional Phase**: Progressive sprint drills starting at 60% velocity, accelerating to 95%+ maximum velocity before full match clearance. Hamstring strength symmetry >95% on hand-held dynamometry.
