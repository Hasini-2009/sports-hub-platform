# Hydration Protocols and Exertional Heat Illness (EHI)

Exertional heat illness ranges from mild heat cramps to life-threatening exertional heat stroke (EHS).

## Spectrum of Heat Disorders
1. **Heat Cramps**: Involuntary painful muscle spasms caused by large water and sodium sweat losses during prolonged strenuous exercise. Treat with sodium-electrolyte fluid replenishment and passive stretching.
2. **Heat Exhaustion**: Inability to continue exercise associated with heavy sweating, tachycardia, weakness, dizziness, headache, nausea, core body temperature <40°C (104°F) without central nervous system dysfunction. Move to shade, elevate legs, remove gear, provide active cooling.
3. **Exertional Heat Stroke (EHS) - MEDICAL EMERGENCY**:
   - Diagnostic criteria: Core temperature >40.5°C (>105°F) AND central nervous system abnormalities (delirium, ataxia, disorientation, collapse, coma).
   - Emergency Treatment: **"Cool First, Transport Second"**. Immediate whole-body cold water immersion (tub with ice and water stirring continuously) until core temperature drops below 38.6°C (101.5°F). Rapid cooling within 30 minutes of collapse saves lives.

## Optimal Pre-, Intra-, and Post-Event Hydration Guidelines
- **Pre-Event (2-4 hours prior)**: Consume 5-10 mL/kg body mass of fluid along with a light sodium-containing snack.
- **During Event**: 0.4 - 0.8 L/hour containing 20-30 mmol/L sodium and 4-8% carbohydrate for events >60 minutes in heat.
- **Post-Event**: Rehydrate with 1.25 to 1.5 L of fluid for every 1 kg of body mass lost during the match. Include electrolytes to prevent exercise-associated hyponatremia.
