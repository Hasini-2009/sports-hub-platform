# Sport-Related Concussion (SRC) and Emergency Red Flags

A concussion is a traumatic brain injury induced by biomechanical forces. It can result from a direct blow to the head, face, neck, or an impulsive force transmitted to the head from an impact elsewhere on the body.

## CRITICAL EMERGENCY RED FLAGS (Call Emergency Services / Seek Immediate Hospital Care)
If an athlete experiences ANY of the following signs after head or body trauma, activate emergency medical transport immediately:
- **Loss of consciousness or deteriorating conscious state**
- **Repeated vomiting or nausea**
- **Severe or increasing headache**
- **Unequal pupil size or diplopia (double vision)**
- **Seizures, convulsions, or muscle twitching**
- **Slurred speech or difficulty understanding spoken words**
- **Weakness, tingling, numbness, or burning sensation in arms or legs**
- **Neck pain or cervical spine tenderness**
- **Clear fluid leaking from nose or ears (CSF leak)**
- **Increasing confusion, irritability, or unusual behavioral changes**

## On-Field Rule: "When in Doubt, Sit Them Out"
- NEVER allow an athlete with a suspected concussion to return to play on the same day.
- Concussion cannot be excluded simply because the athlete did not lose consciousness; >90% of concussions occur without loss of consciousness.
- Remove from play, escort to medical observation, and prohibit driving.

## Graduated Return-to-Sport Strategy (Berlin Consensus)
1. Symptom-limited activity (daily tasks that do not provoke symptoms).
2. Light aerobic exercise (walking, stationary cycling, no resistance).
3. Sport-specific non-contact exercise (running, soccer dribbling, no heading).
4. Non-contact training drills (more complex coordination, progressive resistance).
5. Full-contact practice (only following formal medical physician clearance).
6. Return to competitive play.
Each stage requires a minimum of 24 hours symptom-free before advancing.
