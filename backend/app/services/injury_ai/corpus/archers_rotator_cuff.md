# Shoulder and Forearm Strain Prevention in Competitive Archery

Competitive archery requires extreme muscular endurance, unilateral postural symmetry, and isometric precision. The high draw-weight forces (up to 40-50 lbs on Olympic recurve bows) place repetitive stress on both the bow arm and draw arm.

## Common Pathologies
- **Bow Arm Shoulder**: Supraspinatus and subscapularis impingement from resisting bow force in terminal abduction.
- **Draw Arm Scapula & Spine**: Rhomboid and levator scapulae myofascial pain syndrome and thoracic paraspinal spasms from forceful scapular retraction during anchor holds.
- **Finger Flexor Tenosynovitis**: Irritation of the flexor digitorum tendons from the repetitive bowstring release.

## Preventive Warm-Up & Physical Conditioning
1. **Bow Draw Simulation with Therabands**:
   - 3 sets of 10 controlled draw simulations with elastic bands to prime the posterior deltoid, rhomboids, and middle/lower trapezius prior to shooting real arrows.
2. **Rotator Cuff Conditioning**:
   - Prone horizontal abduction (T-raises) and external rotation in 90 degrees abduction (W-to-Y raises).
3. **Postural Antagonist Work**:
   - Because archery is strictly asymmetric, regularly train bilateral rowing and core anti-rotation (Pallof press) to prevent scoliosis or asymmetric muscle hypertrophy.
4. **Draw Weight Progressive Overload**:
   - Never increase bow draw weight by more than 2-4 lbs at one time, allowing connective tissues 4-6 weeks to adapt to increased poundage.
