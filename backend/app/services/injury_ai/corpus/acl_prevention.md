# Anterior Cruciate Ligament (ACL) Injury Prevention

Anterior Cruciate Ligament (ACL) tears predominantly occur through non-contact mechanisms: sudden deceleration, lateral plant-and-cut maneuvers, or landing from jumps with knees collapsed into dynamic valgus and hip internal rotation.

## Evidence-Based Neuromuscular Training (NMT)
Implementation of structured neuromuscular programs (such as FIFA 11+ or PEP Program) reduces non-contact ACL injuries by up to 50-70% in young athletes.

## Key Program Components
1. **Landing Mechanics**:
   - Land softly on forefoot transitioning to midfoot with flexed knees and hips (avoid stiff, straight-legged landings).
   - Maintain knee alignment over the second toe (prevent knee valgus collapse inward).
2. **Posterior Chain Strengthening**:
   - Hamstring curls, single-leg deadlifts, glute bridges, and hip abductor strengthening (clamshells, banded monster walks).
3. **Core and Trunk Control**:
   - Lateral planks, bird dogs, anti-rotation cable presses. A stable trunk prevents excessive lateral displacement over the support leg during directional changes.
4. **Plyometrics with Quality Feedback**:
   - Box drop landings focusing on knee flexion angle (>30 degrees).
   - Lateral bounding with 2-second stick-landing holds.

## Red Flag Warning
Any sudden 'pop' sensation in the knee accompanied by immediate swelling (hemarthrosis within 2 hours) and feeling of the knee 'giving way' warrants immediate immobilization and prompt MRI evaluation by an orthopedic specialist.
